# StrokePoint++ figure legends

Figure 1 | StrokePoint++ study and implemented application. a-c, Real MRA, supplied binary vessel surface and corresponding labeled point cloud from the first frozen Lausanne DEV case. d, Source-verified frozen PointNet++-derived representation, calibrated identity readout, predicted-segment aggregation, two logistic presence heads and blocked-class reassignment. The heads do not certify pointwise or patient-event safety. e, Original paired CT/MR application, separately reconstructed single-MRA interface and fixed external DEV/FINAL roles. The task conditions on a supplied vessel foreground; neither end-to-end vessel extraction nor clinical stroke prediction has been established. The spatial visual narrative is inspired by Dai et al. (2026), not an implementation of their OT mechanism.

Figure 2 | Source-verified PointNet++ representation and candidate decision. a-c, Label-blind input geometry and fixed anchor subsets from the same real DEV foreground, not learned attention or connectivity labels. The displayed SA1 query has 32 neighborhood slots but only 21 unique source points, including repeated self-padding; it is not 32 distinct neighbors. d, Hierarchical local/context paths, shared full native path descriptors, feature propagation and saved identity readout. F/L descriptor pooling follows the frozen implementation. e, DBSCAN operates on normalized xyz of predicted-class points. Segment descriptors are max-aggregated, standardized by saved training statistics and passed to logistic heads. The application threshold is fixed at 0.05. The original internal version pools paired CT/MR; the reconstructed external version deploys MRA alone and is not the original model state. Reassignment is not an explicit unknown state, and a presence score is not an action-safety probability. No OT, active reading or new joint objective is claimed.

Figure 3 | Original development and internal application evidence. a,b, FP0-X historical clean-benchmark aggregates, with original 95% intervals and effective denominators; development comparisons are exploratory. A recorded historical reviewer-source hash mismatch remains an audit limitation and is not repaired by relabeling these outputs. c,d, Original FP0-Y held-out paired CT/MR application: 65 patients contribute Dice, while 64 with at least one absent native class contribute FPR. FPR changes from 0.125651 to 0.070964, a 43.52% relative reduction. Oracle arms are target-assisted diagnostics, not deployed comparators or information-theoretic limits. These are not the reconstructed single-MRA external model states. No raw data from the protected 65 were accessed for artwork; only existing aggregate JSON was used.

Figure 4 | Locked external single-MRA evaluation. a-d, Four fixed arms on 20 Lausanne DEV and 20 IXI-HH FINAL patients, kept separate rather than pooled. Points and intervals are descriptive patient-level means and arm-wise 95% bootstrap intervals; primary paired family-adjusted contrasts are reported separately in Extended Data Figure 3. FPR is a patient mean over evaluable native class-absence indicators: 20 evaluable DEV patients and 19 FINAL patients. e, The second frozen DEV case, selected by manifest order independently of performance, shown with an identical view and point correspondence. BASE3D and CONCAT share raw-image inputs and short-pilot training scale; Y/Z differs in information and training history, so cross-family rankings do not isolate pure architectural causation. Strong3D is not a fully optimized nnU-Net pipeline. FINAL is source-record-qualified within this study, not proof of globally never-used data.

Figure 5 | Rare-branch performance and explicit correction safety. a, Reviewed FINAL point recall for each method; native-positive supports are 11, 11, 19 and 2 for right Pcom, left Pcom, Acom and third-A2. Small-support undefined intervals remain undetermined. b, Gate relative to its own ungated reconstruction: 8 corrected points, 1 new error and 1 overlapping rare true-positive loss among 31 modified points in 5 patients. These must not be conflated with cross-model discrepancies against BASE3D. c, Formula-based Clopper-Pearson precision curves, not additional patients or experiments. The actual 1/20 event result has a one-sided 95% upper bound of 0.2161, exceeding the unchanged 0.10 cap. d, Application utility and remaining scientific limits. Presence gating is not a reliable action-risk certificate.

Extended Data Figure 1 | Input geometry and conditional task. a,b, The real DEV input and binary vessel surface. c, Native affine, physical coordinate and fixed point-index correspondence are kept explicit; all methods share the same sampled points. d, Full-mask anatomical presence and sampled-point support are distinct. A missing sampled label does not establish anatomical absence. Panels are diagrams of checked operations, not new model experiments.

Extended Data Figure 2 | Complete 13-class external point-recall descriptors. a,b, Lausanne DEV and IXI-HH FINAL remain separate. Entries are reviewed per-patient positive-support recall means, not pooled point accuracy. Class ordering follows the native canonical set 1-12,15. See source data for supports, full-native positives/negatives and interval status. No missing interval is converted into evidence of equivalence.

Extended Data Figure 3 | Prespecified paired external comparisons. a-d, Cohort-specific differences use the original patient-paired bootstrap and simultaneous three-comparison intervals. Negative Dice differences indicate lower point macro-Dice, while negative FPR differences indicate fewer absent-class false detections. FINAL fusion Dice and gate-versus-own improvements have intervals crossing or touching zero. These panels do not change comparators or thresholds after inspecting FINAL.

Extended Data Figure 4 | Support and denominator limits. a, Complete native positive and absent patient counts for all canonical classes in FINAL. b,c, Sparse Acom-negative and third-A2-positive supports preclude precise rare-branch guarantees. One FINAL patient has no absent native class, making overall FPR undefined for that patient; the patient remains in Dice and risk-event analyses. Undefined bootstrap draws are preserved as an undetermined interval, not discarded to create a precise unconditional estimate.

Supplementary Figure 1 | Deterministic real-case point-cloud gallery. The first four frozen Lausanne DEV cases appear in manifest order, independent of outcome. Within each case, reference and all model outputs share points, projection and scale. Colors denote anatomical identity, not model attention. No FINAL case was selected or tuned for this gallery.

Supplementary Figure 2 | Rare-class metric profiles in FINAL. Recall, class Dice and precision use their corresponding eligibility conditions and must not be treated as interchangeable measures. Means with sparse support remain descriptive; the exact confidence-interval status and effective denominators are provided in source data. No aggregate FPR reduction substitutes for rare true-positive preservation.

Supplementary Figure 3 | Version and evidence taxonomy. Original application, newly reconstructed single-MRA transfer, strong baseline, simple fusion and research variants are named separately. Contaminated or implementation-invalid historical results do not support a positive claim, and stopped mechanisms do not become successful through renaming. The code package records executable boundaries, model hashes and third-party terms; it does not redistribute raw patient images, labels or weights.
